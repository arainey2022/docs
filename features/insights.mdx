What good is having hundreds or thousands of customer or user conversations if you can't quickly see what they are saying?

We wanted to make it easy to get deep insights into what your users are asking your AI agent, without you having to read through all the transcripts, manually tag things and generally do a lot of "busy work".

### How to Use Your AI Support Agent's Insights

Here's how our Insights work:

### Conversations

A conversation is a chat that a user has had with an AI agent within a session, or 2.5 messages if you are using one of our integrations (i.e. with [Intercom](/features/channels/intercom), [Zendesk](/features/channels/zendesk/zendesk-messaging) or [HubSpot](/features/channels/hubspot)). Sessions are re-set after 1 hour. 1 conversation or chat can include multiple messages.

You can review and re-read conversations at any time by clicking on a topic and selecting the conversation you want to review.

A conversation is between a visitor and the AI support agent, there is not an allowance per visitor, only at your overall account level.

In the dashboard you will see a number of "Public conversations", these are conversations held with users and are the number of different conversation "[topics](/features/insights#topics)" that the conversations have been broken into.

<Info>
  Any conversations held from the Dashboard or via the Slack or Teams integrations will not contribute to the Insights as they are classed as "Internal" or "Private" conversations.
</Info>

In the bottom left of the Dashboard, you will see "Conversation credits", these may not match your "Public conversations" or tickets/conversations in your messaging platform for a few reasons:

- The Insights dashboard does not capture "[Internal/Private](/features/private-internal-mode)" conversations held
- Conversations via our [Integrations](/features/channels) follow our API pricing, meaning that each question sent by a user counts as 0.4 "conversation credits", or, to put it another way, every 5 questions counts as 2 conversation credits. If a ticket is submitted, the initial ticket response counts as 1 credit and subsequent follow on messages count as 0.4 conversation credits.

<Info>
  We do not charge any conversation credits for "Quick replies" i.e. responses to messages like "Thanks", "Got it", "Sure" or telling a user about accepted question types.
</Info>

### Topics

After each conversation has concluded with an AI agent we use AI to analyze it and break it down into "topics".

Topics are summaries of each issue identified or raised within the conversation, one conversation could contain multiple topics or just a single topic.

Each topic is given a title and description that summarises that topic, along with several tags that can help you quickly get up to speed.

You can dive into each topic by clicking the title, this will open up the detailed Insights page from which you can see all the associated conversations, and also a link to share the topic with a team member.

**Insight notifications**

When a topic reaches 3 conversations, a notification (email or Slack) is sent to the admin and all team members to notify them.

[Learn more about setting up insight notifications](/features/insights/conversation-insight-notifications).

#### Topic visibility

All topics are shown until a topic reaches 3 conversations, at which point only topics with more than 3 conversations will be displayed.

You can view all topics with any number of conversations by clicking _"view all"_ at the top of the Topics table.

#### Categories

Each topic will also be categorized into either a:

- Question
- Bug
- Feedback

This is so you can quickly find and filter on topics of interest to you, questions may require documentation, bugs may require passing to an IT team and feedback to a product team.

### Muting a topic

If you open a topic you can change the status to either "Mark as done :white\_check\_mark:" or "Mute & Hide :no\_bell:", here's what they each mean:

- _Mark as done_ :white\_check\_mark: = Mark as done to permanently close this insight/issue. For new similar conversations, a new insight/issue will be created. Anything marked as "Done" will disappear from your Dashboard. To re-open, you can select _"View all"_ on the topic list, find the closed topic, then change the status.
- _Mute & Hide_ :no\_bell: _=_ Mute and hide this insight/issue from your dashboard. New similar conversations will continue to be added to this insight/issue. Anything "Muted" will disappear from your Dashboard. To unmute you can select _"View all"_ on the topic list, find the closed topic, then change the status.

### AI resolved

This is a flag showing whether the user requested to "Talk to a person" from the conversation. If they didn't then the conversation is marked as being "Resolved by AI".

The AI resolved % is then calculated as the number of conversations resolved by AI / total number of conversations.

### AI CSAT

We use a separate AI model to assess conversations and tickets.

You can see the CSAT scoring section of the prompt below (based on the [AXIS framework](https://front.com/blog/ai-experience-impact-score-axis)):

```text
Review the provided conversation between an AI agent labeled as "assistant" and a customer labeled as "user", and assess the quality and outcome of the interaction based on specific criteria.

Consider the following elements during your assessment:
- Analyze the overall satisfaction and outcome of the conversation for the user.

# Steps
1. **Validate Conversation**
Before performing steps 2 and 3 you must check whether to provide a CSAT score (`ai_csat`). You must assign a CSAT score of "null" (and you can ignore step 2) IF:
     - The user's message is blank or clearly doesn't ask a question.
     - The user's message looks like a spam email or spam message.
     - The conversation consists of a single user question that is responded to by the assistant with a clarifying question that the user does not respond to.

2. **Assess Satisfaction**
   Assign an appropriate CSAT score (`ai_csat`) with a generous, outcome-focused assessment of the main help the user requested. Give credit for a useful answer and a workable solution. Assess the main purpose of the conversation rather than counting every minor omission.
   - **1:** Very dissatisfied. The main issue remains unresolved and the assistant clearly caused or increased the user's negative tone.
   - **2:** Dissatisfied. Use 2 when a material failure in the assistant's handling leaves the main request blocked and the customer still needs a person to resolve it. If the customer says the help failed, repeats the same unmet request after unusable help, or asks for a person because the help failed, the later transfer does not erase that failure. Keep the lower score unless the assistant corrects the material failure or supplies the missing help. A routine request for a person or frustration with the original problem is insufficient on its own.
   - **3:** Neutral. Use 3 when there is a specific material shortfall in the main assistance. A generic offer to escalate without a usable route or a concrete next action for this request is partial help. Use 3 if a material part remains unanswered or the reply is irrelevant, empty, or repeatedly asks for clarification without useful help. Silence, no thanks, or the absence of an outcome update is insufficient on its own. Apply step 1 for a single unanswered clarifying question.
   - **4:** Satisfied. Give 4 when the assistant answers the main request adequately or provides a credible, usable next step. A planned transfer can itself earn 4 when the conversation shows why a person needs to act and the assistant clearly states a next action that fits the main request. The next action can be a direct transfer to a team able to act on this request or concrete directions to the appropriate route. A named department is optional when the purpose and path are clear. Give credit for a stated transfer as a next step; claim completion only when the chat shows it. A generic offer or vague referral with no usable path is insufficient. When earlier help failed materially and that failure remains uncorrected, apply the lower score even if a transfer is later offered. Keep 4 for an otherwise adequate answer followed by silence or a routine external action.
   - **5:** Very satisfied. The main request is fully and clearly addressed with no material unresolved part, and either the user subsequently shows positive acceptance or the assistant supplies a complete, direct and usable answer that requires no further explanation from the assistant. A complete factual answer, a clear process explanation or specific workable instructions can qualify for 5 without a thank-you or confirmation of completion. Do not require enthusiastic language. Do not use 5 for generic advice, an unresolved handover or an answer the user says did not help.
   - Prefer 4 over 3 for useful assistance with only minor or uncertain gaps. Prefer 5 over 4 when the main answer is clear and complete. Reserve 3 and below for concrete shortcomings, not imagined dissatisfaction.
   - Consider the user's initial frustration only if the assistant made it worse. Do not invent a user reaction, completed action or confirmed outcome. Describe useful assistance and any assumed resolution accurately in the score reason.

3. **Provide Reason for CSAT Score**
   Write a succinct one-sentence justification (`ai_csat_reason`) for the CSAT score assigned.
   - Ensure the reasoning includes the key reason contributing to that score (e.g., solution quality, tone of user response, escalation).
   - You must **always provide a CSAT score reason** (even if the AI CSAT score is null (from Step 1))

# Output Format

The output must be in JSON with the following fields:
- `ai_csat` (Integer, range: 1 to 5, or null)
- `ai_csat_reason` (String, one sentence describing the justification)


# Notes
- Pay careful attention to user sentiment and any explicit requests to determine the overall interaction quality.
- The CSAT score should be consistent with the user's satisfaction level at the conclusion of the conversation.
```

Conversations that do not meet the eligibility rules above receive no score. Your dashboard's AI CSAT percentage is the share of scored conversations rated 4 or 5.

You can view your AI CSAT score in the Dashboard and for each individual topic and conversation by clicking on the respective conversation.

You can also see the rationale for the CSAT score by clicking on an individual conversation.

### Date

The date refers to the date the 1st conversation was held on a topic.

### Time saved

The time saved is calculated by estimating the time saved per conversation, multiplied by the number of conversations (which were resolved by AI).

You can edit the time saved per conversation by going to your Account Settings, by default it is set to 5 minutes.

### Resolution times

Each conversation is timed from the time of the 1st question asked to the time of the last question or the point of human handover.

This time is denoted as the "resolution time" and can be found for each individual conversation.

The median resolution time across all conversations can also be found on the main dashboard.

### Historic data (Logs)

At any time you can see the historic Insights data for your AI agent by using the period dropdown to see data from the last day, week, month, quarter, half year and year.

This will allow you to see how many conversations your AI agent has had over different periods, how your AI resolution rate is changing over time and how much time you are saving each day, week or month.

Note, that this data is for the last period from the date you have selected, you cannot currently choose to see data between specific historic dates unless you use the Insights Export feature.

### Export Conversation History and Questions (Logs)

To export Conversation History or Question data to Excel or Google Sheets, you can click the export button by going to **Insights \> Explorer** and then clicking the Export button at the top right.

<Info>
  For best results, import the Exported data to Google Sheets, as Excel has a tendency to not be able to parse/read special characters.
</Info>

This will allow you to choose to export Conversation History or Questions between the two dates you choose into a CSV file that you can use for further analysis.

Currently for each conversation you will be able to see:

- [Category](/features/insights#categories)
- AI CSAT
- Description
- Human handover: TRUE means the conversation was passed to a live agent
- Original conversation
- Parent conversation topic
- Parent topic
- Total conversation topics count
- Resolution time
- Resolved
- Source: Where the conversation with the user was held (e.g. Intercom, Dashboard, Slack)
- Status
- Title
- Creation
- Date
- Unique ID

### Filtering and sorting

By default topics are sorted by number of conversations, and show for all categories. However, you can filter on the Category (Question, Bug, Feedback), or sort from high to low on:

- Number of conversations
- % AI resolved

<Info>
  The maximum export period for questions is 30 days. Should you need longer than this, contact support and ask to speak to a person.
</Info>

### Charts

In both your main Dashboard page and your individual topics' Insights pages, you will see charts so you can track activity over time.

On the Dashboard, you can see: AI resolution rates/AI CSAT and Conversation volumes over time, switching between them using the toggle switch in the bottom right hand side of the chart.

On the detailed Insights page you will see a chart showing how often that particular topic has been raised over time, helping you identify peaks and troughs in activity.

All data is calculated nightly, so you will see conversations summarised from the previous day only.

## Using Echo Agent with Insights

Your [Echo AI agent](/features/echo-agent) has access to all the data and conversations you see in your Insights and is the easiest way to understand more about your AI agent's performance.

Just ask the questions you want the answers to, it could be:

- _What were our top 3 problems last week?_
- _Why did AI resolution drop on 23 May?_
- _What are the top 3 things I can do to improve AI resolution?_

But it isn't limited to these, ask whatever you want.

It can also create charts to demonstrate its findings and help you decide on the next step.
